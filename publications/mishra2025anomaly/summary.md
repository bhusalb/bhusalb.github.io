## Abstract

Wireless Sensor Networks form the backbone of modern cyber physical systems used in applications such as environmental monitoring, healthcare monitoring, industrial automation, and smart infrastructure. In this paper a lightweight and interpretable anomaly detection framework based on a first order Markov chain model is proposed. The method discretizes continuous sensor readings into finite states and models the temporal dynamics of sensor transitions through a transition probability matrix. Anomalies are detected when observed transitions occur with probabilities below a computed threshold, allowing for real time detection without labeled data or intensive computation. The framework is validated on the Intel Berkeley Research Lab dataset, and comparative analysis with Z score, Hidden Markov Model, and autoencoder based methods shows a balanced trade-off between accuracy, interpretability, and computational efficiency.
